Signify

Smart lighting / consumer tech · UK & Europe · 2022–2024 (Merkle)

Leading media strategy across six European markets

My role Senior Strategic Planning Manager at Merkle, Media Strategy Lead on the Signify account across key European markets

Signify is the world’s largest lighting company. The global team owned the brand framework. My responsibility was turning the framework into a working strategy for the UK, DACH, Benelux and the Nordics as the main markets, with Italy and Spain at smaller scale, while the smart lighting category was shrinking and brand investment was reduced quarter by quarter.

~€1.9M
annual media budget for one market alone
1.9% lift
UK YouTube awareness, 24% cheaper CPLU
1.3× ROAS
Performance Max vs text ads

Approach

Keeping the brand coherent across markets of different size. In the UK I costed the audience at each stage of the funnel and focused on the top, putting about 1.4 times more into awareness than the client had recommended. The main job was to grow the number of people entering the “funnel”, not to work harder on retention.
Optimizing the global audience approach for more effective reach. The audience data showed ~30% overlap between personas. From late 2022 the top of the funnel was consolidated across every market into one shared audience with one brand message, while persona and proposition work moved down the funnel.
Running brand communication despite the budget cuts. Competitor tracking showed that almost all paid activity was focused on Q4 with long periods of media silence, so campaigns were launched ahead of competitors and small-budget bursts went into the weeks when nobody else was active.
Aligning the whole team around the strategy. Channel teams were briefed at every stage with the strategic context and the client’s feedback, not only the task. The strategy was executed holistically, not as a collection of plans.

Impact

  • A continuous, competitive voice on a smaller budget.
  • Roughly six campaign moments per market each year over a permanent always-on layer.
  • On our own competitive tracking, Hue held the strongest organic visibility in the category throughout the period.
  • Led and coordinated cross-functional teams of up to seven channel specialists and third-party media owners.

WWF-UK

Charity / conservation · UK · 2023–2024 (Merkle)

Proving the case for brand investment in a performance-led fundraising business

My role Senior Strategic Planning Manager at Merkle, one of two strategists on the WWF-UK account

The main goal of the project was to invest in the brand by building effective reach and keeping the brand relevant in communication, while maintaining the effectiveness of performance campaigns. The client entered the next year with consistent brand & fundraising architecture, optimized audience strategy and tested communication solutions.

7×
potential size of the target donor audience
+22%
branded paid search clicks from a cultural-moment campaign
+10%
above fundraising target in the most competitive period

The problem

  • We were working with demand that already existed: pure brand terms brought 21% of all LTV and took only 6% of the search budget.
  • Brand demand itself had been falling for more than one year; awareness declined by around 7 percentage points over five years.
  • Existing digital audiences were becoming saturated.
  • WWF was behind the category on media scale.
  • Communication in the category often looked the same.
  • The cost-of-living crisis was narrowing the wallet.

Solutions & impact

A complete audience strategy. WWF’s segments were rebuilt, sized and deduplicated. Within a test campaign the broader segment group achieved a CPM around 1.5 times lower on average.
Distinctive assets and donor behavior. In testing, the algorithm gave 94% of the budget to expressive video with storytelling, and the only British animal in the set took 68% of the budget and delivered a matching 69% of leads.
Salience and relevance through context. A campaign tied to a cultural moment lifted branded paid search: +18% impressions and +22% clicks. The test creative took 29% of the month’s conversions at a CPA 10% below the campaign average.
The case for brand investment. Binet and Field’s work on long-term effects, consistent with the brand’s own local econometrics, helped to convince the client to buy reach across the whole base continuously rather than in bursts.

Mercedes-Benz Ukraine

Automotive · Ukraine · 2015–2018 · in-house

Holding premium positioning through price challenges

My role Brand Communication Manager (in-house)

After 2014 Ukraine’s car market went through tough times. An imported premium car became much more expensive in local currency. In 2015 Mercedes-Benz sold only ~1,430 cars — almost the same as its main competitor, BMW. So, my task was not only to adapt a brand with strict global guidelines and strategy to local audience specifics, but also to keep its premium positioning in a segment that had to compete on discounts.

1,430 → 2,897
cars sold, 2015 → 2017
#1
in the premium segment by 2017, ahead of BMW
Up to 10
national campaigns a year

Approach

Price messaging without repositioning. The sales department ran clearance campaigns once or twice a year. Discounts bring short-term sales but don’t sustain growth and are harmful to premium perception. So, I kept the core brand message while testing price appeal with softer wording like “special offers” or “attractive prices, ask for details” and ran tests to choose the one that performed better.
Media decisions based on post-campaign analysis. I planned every digital campaign based on previous channel metrics and Google Analytics data, and moved budget to what worked. Programmatic and Meta Ads first entered the mix. As a result, cost per reach fell and site engagement improved.
Optimized placement through negotiation. Quality placements at controlled cost, agreed directly with agencies and media owners instead of paying standard rates.
National consistency across a dealer network. I collected local business input from dealer marketing teams across Ukraine for national campaigns and tracked that their webpages, adverts and socials were aligned with the brand guidelines.

Impact

  • Annual campaign budget around UAH 8–12M.
  • ~800K paid reach at frequency 6 and ~18K site visits per campaign (3–4 weeks long).
  • ~250 cars a month converted by the dealer network at peak.
  • Kept the position ahead of BMW in 2018, when Audi disrupted the premium segment with heavily discounted diesel stock.
